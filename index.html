Why This Project?

Through the course of Media Aesthetics II, I have come to an understanding that text is built on a foundation of cultural and learned meaning. In other words, text in itself doesn't hold significant meaning. This has made the practice of translation far more ambiguous to me--if the signifiers are arbitrary and entirely built on a culture of understanding, how can a foreign culture's language perfectly capture it's essence? Therefore, for my final project, I decided to task myself with experimenting with two concepts: practicing translation as a procedural task with rules and practicing translation with the transformer ["computer language"] as the intermediary.

Translation as a procedural task has already been a professional practice for centuries. One of the most challenging domains of translation is found in poetry (as learned through class with Du Fu and a multitude of translations of his work). I challenged myself to capture and train a model to procedurally perform translations using specific guidelines that are built through creating a fine-tuning training dataset. The greatest challenges of this task were: finding proper training data, formatting and cleaning training data, and maintaining translation quality without having the LLM regurgitate existing translations. Without getting too technical, training data was creataed by making 1-10 translations for every word in 10 dictionaries (either from Chinese-Enlish Korean-English, or Chinese-Korean). These lists were verified using a script that called a GPT API. The procedures (which are detailed under each translation under "Translation Model Methodology") were inspired from translators such as Bynner from the Du Fu translation packet given in class. In order to prevent the LLMs from copying existing translation, before the model releases a response, a GAN (an opposing model) compares the generated translation to the top 30 translation queries found on Google. Given a +40% token match and a +80% semantics match, the response is rejected and the model is prompted to create another translation with greater randomness by increasing the temperature.

A topic that has interested me since learning about text in Media Aesthetics is how LLMs can inform our understanding of language in an objective way. LLMs, like humans, understand language as signifiers. However, unique to humans, they do not have comprehension of their signifiers. Since LLMs are entirely trained on text data extracted from the internet (and even images being reduced to numbers that they must find patterns in), their perception of language is totally abstract. Following that line of logic, LLMs in the act of translation could provide insight that cannot be found in people. Because a lot of human translation lies in associating how different signifiers refer to different signified objects, humans are partially distracted by the real world to create purly textual translation. However, LLMs are fully reliant on text--all the association an LLM makes is based on associations of words. Studying how LLMs navigate translation can reveal hidden connections between language and words that can only be seen through a brain that thinks exclusively with text--and That is why translation of poetry through LLMs is compelling to me.
